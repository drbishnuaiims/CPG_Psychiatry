01 · Foundation

The big picture

PD

A “snowflake” disease

Parkinson’s is a neurodegenerative disease with combinations of motor and non-motor symptoms that differ in severity and progression from person to person.

TremorStiffnessSlownessWalkingMoodCognitionFatigue
D

Dopamine is central

Dopamine depletion and impaired dopamine release contribute to the motor symptoms of Parkinsonism — particularly tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity and walking problems.

QOL

Practical treatment goal

The current day-to-day goal is symptom improvement and quality of life. Disease-stopping or disease-slowing treatments remain a research goal.

Educational framing: The briefing was intended as general information, not individualized medical recommendations. Treatment decisions should be individualized with the person’s neurologist/healthcare team.

Severity is a spectrum

Very early
~5 years before to ~5 years after diagnosis
Mild symptoms may not interfere with daily activities; medication may not yet be necessary for some people.
Mild
Diagnosis to ~10 years
Symptoms increasingly interfere with activities, often with a good smooth medication response.
Moderate
~5–20 years
Medication responses become less adequate/shorter; therapeutic window narrows; disability, balance and gait issues can accumulate.
Advanced
~10–20+ years
More fluctuations, freezing, falls and non-motor complications; dementia may occur in some people.

Medication philosophy

Use medications when symptoms are meaningfully affecting quality of life.

Use the lowest amount needed to obtain the desired benefit while limiting adverse effects.

Treatment should evolve as the disease and the person’s priorities evolve.

Lifestyle, rehabilitation and education remain important at every stage.

02 · Pharmacology

The medication toolbox

The briefing emphasized that levodopa remains the most effective core treatment for Parkinsonian motor symptoms, while several other classes can be useful in selected situations.

Why levodopa remains “gold standard”

It directly supplies the precursor that is converted to dopamine. In the comparative study shown in the briefing, levodopa produced greater motor benefit than the dopamine agonist used for comparison.

Key principle: if symptoms are reducing quality of life, delaying levodopa simply to “save” it for later can mean losing a year of symptomatic benefit.

Young vs older patients

The speaker described a practical age-sensitive approach: in younger people with mild symptoms, a dopamine agonist or anticholinergic may sometimes be considered selectively; if benefit is inadequate, levodopa can be introduced.

For older patients, the practical tendency is more often levodopa-first, given tolerability and cognitive considerations.

Levodopa does not “wear out” because the medication becomes weak. The briefing emphasized that motor fluctuations are more closely related to progression of Parkinson’s and the brain’s changing ability to buffer dopamine, rather than the drug simply losing its intrinsic effectiveness.

Why might levodopa seem not to work?

• Dose may be too low.
• The symptom may be less levodopa-responsive.
• A non-motor symptom such as fatigue may have another cause.
• Gastrointestinal absorption may be inconsistent.
• The duration of benefit may be too short.
• Side effects may limit dose escalation.
• The diagnosis may be atypical if there is not a robust response at an adequate dose.

• Individual body size, genetics, absorption and other factors affect response.
• Walking and balance can become less dopamine-responsive later.
• The therapeutic threshold differs among people.
• Keeping a medication diary can help the clinician identify the actual pattern.

03 · Practical use

Getting the most from levodopa

01

Be consistent

Take medication at the prescribed times rather than repeatedly changing the schedule independently. Consistency makes it possible to understand what is helping and what is causing side effects.

02

Keep a diary

Record dose times, when medication starts working, when it wears off, dyskinesia, sleep and other symptoms. A consistent pattern gives the clinician something actionable to adjust.

03

Think about meals

Levodopa is absorbed in the GI tract. The briefing discussed taking it away from food when tolerated, and avoiding protein around doses when food is necessary because protein can compete with absorption.

Individualization matters: Some people become nauseated on an empty stomach and need a small amount of food. Dietitians can help design a schedule that preserves adequate nutrition and protein while optimizing medication timing.

Carbidopa and nausea

Nausea is a common levodopa adverse effect. The carbidopa component reduces peripheral conversion of levodopa and thereby helps with nausea. The speaker explained the historical association of the name “Sinemet” with “no nausea.”

Formulations

Immediate-release carbidopa-levodopa is commonly used early. Longer-acting formulations can become attractive as dosing frequency increases. The speaker described considering extended-release preparations when a person is needing medication more than roughly three times daily, particularly as convenience and stability become important.

04 · Disease evolution

Wearing-off, “on”, “off” and dyskinesia

What changes over time?

Early in Parkinson’s, levodopa often provides a smooth and sustained benefit. With progression, the response can become shorter and less efficient.

Carbidopa-levodopa has a short pharmacokinetic half-life (the briefing gave approximately 90 minutes for immediate-release Sinemet), while the clinical benefit can initially last considerably longer.

The “bumpy road”

Off: Parkinson symptoms return as medication effect fades.

On: Medication is working, sometimes with excessive involuntary movement.

Dyskinesia: Abnormal extra movement that can look like fidgeting, dancing or restless movement.

Strategies for wearing-off and dyskinesia

StrategyConcept
Fractionate the doseGive smaller doses more frequently to maintain benefit without simply increasing the total daily amount. Example discussed: moving from two tablets four times daily toward one-and-a-half tablets five times daily.
Longer-acting levodopaExtended/delayed-release formulations can lengthen coverage and simplify frequent dosing.
Food timingOptimize GI absorption and reduce food/protein interference when feasible.
Add adjunctive medicationDopamine agonist, MAO-B inhibitor, COMT inhibitor or A2A antagonist can extend levodopa effect; increased dyskinesia can be the trade-off.
On-demand rescueInhaled levodopa or injectable apomorphine can provide an occasional boost rather than changing the entire daily regimen.
Amantadine ERThe briefing described extended-release amantadine as an option that can improve off time while also reducing dyskinesia in appropriate patients.

How common are motor complications?

The speaker cited an approximate pattern of ~50% by five years and ~90% by 10+ years experiencing motor complications such as fluctuations/dyskinesia. The emphasis was that these are related more to disease duration/severity and individual factors than simply to the calendar date levodopa was started.

Off time vs dyskinesia

The majority of people in the speaker’s experience are more bothered by off-time Parkinson symptoms than by dyskinesia. The right target is the person’s own “sweet spot”: enough levodopa to function well without unacceptable involuntary movement.

Morning “off”: The Q&A discussed selected severe early-morning immobility where a family member may give an early dose around 4–5 AM so that the person can become mobile by their usual waking time. This is a clinician-directed strategy, not a universal recommendation.
05 · Dyskinesia

When the medication itself causes movement

Recognize it

Dyskinesia may appear as choreiform, fidgeting or “dancing” movements involving limbs or trunk, often during medication “on” periods.

First approach

If dyskinesia is troublesome, reducing the individual levodopa dose can help. The goal is not automatically to remove all movement, but to find the most functional balance.

Amantadine

Amantadine is the key medication discussed for levodopa-induced dyskinesia. Immediate-release and extended-release forms were mentioned; older people require particular caution for confusion/hallucinations.

Amantadine safety points from the briefing: hallucinations, cognitive problems, sleep disruption, leg swelling and livedo reticularis/skin changes. Avoid unnecessarily late dosing when sleep is affected. Withdrawal should be gradual rather than abrupt.
06 · Whole-person care

Parkinson’s is more than dopamine

The briefing repeatedly emphasized that motor and non-motor symptoms must be balanced. Dopamine can help some symptoms while worsening others.

M

Mood

Depression, anxiety and apathy are common. SSRIs and medications such as mirtazapine were mentioned, along with counseling, exercise and mind-body activities.

A

Autonomic symptoms

Orthostatic hypotension, sweating, drooling/runny nose, erectile dysfunction, constipation and urinary symptoms may require separate treatment.

S

Sleep & fatigue

Sleep quality can strongly influence next-day physical function. Sleep hygiene and cognitive-behavioral approaches may be useful; treatment depends on the specific sleep problem.

C

Cognition & hallucinations

Parkinson medications can worsen hallucinations/confusion in susceptible people. Medication reduction may sometimes be necessary, followed by selected treatments for cognition or psychosis.

B

Balance & freezing

These can become less responsive to dopamine later. Physical therapy, balance training, dance, Tai Chi and cueing devices may help.

G

GI & bladder

Constipation and urinary frequency/urgency/nocturia are common and should be treated as problems in their own right rather than assuming more dopamine is the answer.

The dopamine tug-of-war: More dopamine may improve tremor, dexterity, stiffness and mobility, but can worsen hallucinations, confusion or low blood pressure. In some situations, the safer choice is to lower Parkinson medication even if a motor symptom remains.

Orthostatic hypotension: approaches discussed

Non-drug: hydration, increased salt when appropriate, compression stockings and general medical optimization.

Medication examples: midodrine, fludrocortisone and droxidopa were mentioned as options that may be used under medical supervision.

07 · Lifestyle

Non-medication treatment is not optional

Aerobic activity

Regular aerobic exercise was presented as part of a broad Parkinson’s exercise prescription.

Strength

Strength-building activity complements aerobic work and helps preserve physical function.

Balance

Balance training becomes particularly important as gait and postural stability are affected.

Mind-body practice

Tai Chi, yoga, dance, improv/creative activities and other enjoyable mind-body practices can address motor and non-motor domains together.

Social + goal-based

Choose activities that are enjoyable, social, appropriately challenging and ideally supervised by instructors familiar with Parkinson’s.

General health

Sleep, diet, mental health and management of other medical conditions can substantially influence Parkinson’s function.

Exercise prescription from the briefing: combine aerobic activity + strength + balance training. Goal-based learning of a new skill can benefit both physical and cognitive function.

Cognitive health

The speaker referenced research in older adults suggesting that the combination of diet, exercise, cognitive training and vascular risk-factor reduction is associated with better cognitive function over time.

08 · Advanced therapies

When oral medication is no longer enough

The goal of advanced therapy is to make the “bumpy road” smoother when fluctuations become difficult despite optimized oral regimens.

Continuous levodopa through the gut

Duopa/Duodopa was described as a surgically placed pump delivering levodopa continuously into the gut, reducing reliance on repeated oral pulses.

Subcutaneous infusion

Newer subcutaneous approaches can deliver levodopa-based therapy or apomorphine continuously through the skin. This avoids exclusive reliance on GI absorption but requires training, device management and skin care.

Subcutaneous foslevodopa/foscarbidopa

The briefing described study results showing roughly 3 hours of improved off time versus oral levodopa and nearly 4 hours more medication-on time without troublesome dyskinesia, with improved morning control in people receiving continuous overnight therapy.

Practical limitations

Potential skin irritation/infection, pump management, home nursing, carrying the device, medication filling and an initiation session to determine settings were highlighted.

Deep brain stimulation (DBS)

For selected people with advanced Parkinson’s who remain levodopa-responsive but have disabling fluctuations/dyskinesia or medication limitations. DBS can be adjusted and reversed and can treat both sides and multiple motor symptoms.

Dementia is an important contraindication/caution because DBS is brain surgery and cognition can worsen.

Focused ultrasound

An incisionless lesioning procedure. At the time described in the briefing, the main approved use discussed was unilateral tremor control in Parkinson’s disease or essential tremor.

It requires suitable skull characteristics and is not adjustable/reversible like DBS.

09 · Research

Where the field is heading

Genetics

PD GENEration was highlighted as a Parkinson’s Foundation initiative offering genetic testing and counseling for eligible people with Parkinson’s, with the goal of identifying biological targets and advancing research.

Alpha-synuclein

Alpha-synuclein accumulation is a major research target. Strategies include preventing accumulation, clearing the protein or otherwise altering its disease-related effects.

Stem cells

Another research direction is replacing/regenerating dopamine-producing neurons — effectively attempting to repair the “factory” rather than continuously supplement dopamine.

Hope + realism: The speaker emphasized that current treatments primarily improve symptoms; disease-modifying therapies remain an active research goal and clinical trials take time.
